# Vision — HackJudge AI

## Vision Statement

**Every hackathon submission gets a fair, evidence-based, consistently applied
evaluation — and every judge gets their time back.**

HackJudge AI envisions hackathon judging where scores are transparent,
explainable, and comparable across teams; where judges spend their attention on
insight rather than bookkeeping; and where AI assists the process without ever
replacing human judgment.

## What Success Looks Like

- A judge reviews a submission in minutes, not tens of minutes, without losing
  depth.
- Two judges applying the same rubric reach broadly similar scores — and when
  they diverge, the system surfaces *why*.
- Every suggested score comes with an explanation a human can accept, reject, or
  override in one click.
- Teams can see exactly which criteria they were judged on and why they received
  the scores they did.
- Organizers configure a new event's rubric in minutes and trust that the same
  rules apply to every team.

## Values Embedded in the Vision

1. **Human authority.** AI recommends; humans decide. Final results always
   require human review.
2. **Evidence over claims.** Verified repository/demo evidence is distinguished
   from team-provided claims and AI inferences.
3. **Consistency with flexibility.** One rubric per judging category, but
   organizers customize criteria and weights per event.
4. **Honest uncertainty.** Missing information is "unknown", never a
   punishment; AI confidence is stated, not implied.
5. **Respect for participants.** No inference of team ability or project
   quality from personal or demographic characteristics.

## Long-Term Direction

- From text-only submissions → multimodal analysis (repos, slides, documents,
  recorded demos).
- From single-event judging → multi-event, multi-category judging seasons.
- From manual leaderboard export → published, verifiable results with audit
  trails.
- From ad-hoc follow-up questions → AI-generated, criterion-targeted questions
  that help judges probe deeper.
- From per-hackathon setup → reusable rubric templates shared across
  communities.

## Non-Goals

- Fully automatic judging without human judges.
- Ranking individual team members or assessing participant ability.
- Replacing hackathon organizers' event design decisions.
- Executing or trusting submitted code as part of analysis.
